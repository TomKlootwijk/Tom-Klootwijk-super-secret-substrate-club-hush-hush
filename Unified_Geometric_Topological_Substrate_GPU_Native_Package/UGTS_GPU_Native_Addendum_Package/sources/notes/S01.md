# S01 — Glyph-to-bit, 100% and timeline addendum

## Source-derived mechanisms
- Standard semantics: 100% is the number 1; the source itself eventually restates that boundary.
- Custom visual encoder: treat `100` as a binary glyph, decompose `%` into loop/slash anatomy, remove or leave open the terminal loop under the stated rule, and produce a second `100` pulse. Concatenation yields `100100₂ = 36₁₀`.
- Pulse interpretation: the two `100` groups are two events separated by three bit positions on a 1D address/timeline.
- Scaled 5–12–13 triangle: `1.5, 3.6, 3.9`; leg ratio `3.6/1.5 = 2.4`; cone angle `atan(2.4) ≈ 67.38°`.
- Log-base-2 radial LUT, SDF guard, one-bit jitter band, BST-style routing, toroidal/poloidal labels, trident convergence and Pythagorean-cup threshold/reset.

## Technical normalization
The glyph rule is an explicit encoder, not ordinary percentage arithmetic. The two pulse positions are represented as `(1u<<5) | (1u<<2)`. A fractional factor of 1.5 is a scale or `log2(1.5)` shift, not a native integer bit shift. The Pythagorean cup is normalized as a hysteretic threshold/reset component. Claims of an absolute vacuum, self-sustaining energy recycling, or hundreds of joules derived from information bits are rejected.
