# Source analysis for UGTS-KC 3.6

## Method

The upgrade preserves the source corpus's strongest architecture: finite grammar, typed state, local support, compatibility, relation guards, verified events, explicit transitions, lineage and irreducible external logging. It does not carry forward unrelated narrative subjects. Each motif was classified as exact, reusable engineering translation, bounded mnemonic, corrected statement or rejected extrapolation.

## Reusable tricks extracted from SRC-A

1. **Radix threshold as a scale event.** Digit-count changes occur at powers of the radix. The exact operator is useful for hierarchy and shell indexing.
2. **Active-bit filtering.** Set-bit positions and Hamming weight form a sparse feature descriptor. Removing zero symbols must never silently change the numeric value.
3. **Count-to-simplex embedding.** A declared count of three can be embedded as three vertices. The triangle is a chosen geometry, not a theorem that the original number is triangular.
4. **Pascal parity.** Binomial parity yields a Sierpinski pattern. The statement that generic rows at powers of two are all odd is corrected: with zero-based indexing, rows `2^k - 1` are all odd.
5. **Local log-polar addressing.** Radial multiplication becomes additive displacement in logarithmic radius, with an explicit singular core.
6. **One-bit jitter as an output mask or dither.** It may redistribute quantization error but cannot guarantee perfect anti-aliasing or infinite resolution.
7. **SDF/implicit boundary.** A zero surface is useful as a relation guard. Generic implicit fields are not automatically exact signed-distance fields.
8. **Hinge language.** A hinge is made technical only when its pivot, state, map, guard and invariant are declared.
9. **Dutch spoken-order reversal.** This is the main new 3.6 extraction. Decimal place order and Dutch number-word order are separate charts. Regular teens place the unit root before `tien`; non-multiple tens from 21 through 99 use `unit + en + tens`. The connector `en` has no place magnitude but is structurally necessary in the spoken graph.
10. **Cross-domain coincidence discipline.** A binary popcount may equal a phonetic pulse count. The equality is stored as metadata with a profile and tokenizer, not promoted to numeric identity or physical law.

## Tricks retained from SRC-B and SRC-C

- Boundary-graph cut/unroll and loop-to-open-path morphs.
- Explicit sign conventions, CSG composition and finite guard bands.
- Local cone, sphere and shell support before expensive relation work.
- Co-located states on different sheets or with incompatible phase/orientation.
- Mobius/Klein quotient maps with explicit orientation and sheet transport.
- Four-sector hourglass routing at a central event locus.
- One-bit parity as route, validity or latch state only.
- Schema-bound packed data, content hashes, lineage and novelty logs.
- Precision and error margins as correctness contracts rather than compression claims.

## Claims not carried forward

The following are excluded from the v3.6 technical core: one-bit complete state, zero memory/latency/energy, generic exact non-iterative SDF intersections, infinite resolution, guaranteed alias elimination, arbitrary personal-number correspondences, historical/geological conclusions inferred from decimal patterns, physical conservation laws inferred from a graphics metaphor, and topology as a source of free energy or self-assembly.
